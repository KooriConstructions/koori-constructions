## Part 1 — The strategy answer and the ladder

### The question
Wait and buy the owner-occupier house first, build equity through renovation and growth, then use that equity for the company to get a development loan — or go straight at a knockdown-rebuild duplex and rinse-repeat?

### The answer: both, in one move
Buy the owner-occupier house first — **but buy it as a duplex site, not as a house.** The selection criteria are the development feasibility, not the kitchen:

- ≥500m² in Lake Macquarie (Torrens split to 250m²/side), or ≥450m² with 12m frontage inside a Low & Mid-Rise ring (Adamstown, Kotara, Hamilton, Cardiff, Morisset, Teralba, Booragul, Gosford, Wyong, Woy Woy station catchments; Charlestown, Belmont, Mayfield, The Junction, Raymond Terrace centres), or ≥300–450m²-per-lot combined-DA territory in Maitland/R1.
- Flat or gently sloping, sewer not through the build zone, corner or 12m+ frontage preferred (corner blocks can satisfy the 12m rule off the secondary frontage).
- The worst house on the street — priced as a renovator by owner-occupier agents, valued as land in your residual model.

**Why this beats going straight to a company development loan:**

1. **Cost of money.** Owner-occupier lending: highest LVR (90–95% possible), lowest rates, and your ~$500k serviceability actually works there. Development finance needs 20–30% of total development cost in cash equity and prices at roughly 9–12%+ with private lenders. You currently lack the cash equity — that's the whole problem the PPOR solves.
2. **Equity manufacturing.** You renovate at cost. A $60–80k cosmetic-plus reno that would cost a civilian $120k+ can add $150k+ of valuation in the right suburb. Revalue, release equity — that release is the deposit for site #2 or the equity in your own KDR duplex facility.
3. **The block is the pipeline.** When you're ready, the PPOR itself can become the first duplex (KDR via the CDC pattern-book pathway — ~10-day approvals — or DA), with one crucial caveat: **developing your own main residence has CGT/GST traps** (you can lose the main-residence exemption; profits go on revenue account). Get specialist tax advice *before* demolishing — Part 6 §2.
4. **Track record for lenders.** "Licensed builder, owns home with equity, builds duplex with own hands" is instantly financeable. "New company, no equity, first project" is not.

**What the $500k realistically buys:** at 88–90% LVR with LMI, a purchase around $550–650k plus costs. That's real duplex-block territory in Teralba/Booragul/Morisset catchments (LMR rings), Wyong, Raymond Terrace, Cessnock (600m²+ only), and parts of Maitland/Thornton — not Charlestown/Adamstown at ~$1M medians. Buy the growth corridor you can afford; the LMR ring does the heavy lifting.

**Do these in parallel from day one (they cost almost nothing):**
- **Structure advice now, before any contract** — SPV-per-project convention, builder entity separate from asset entity, family home quarantined from guarantees. Restructuring after purchase triggers duty/CGT.
- **Start the brand now** — document builds on Instagram, Graya-style. Every founder story in this research says brand early; media is a financing instrument (presale databases are built years before the launch).
- **Run feasos weekly** as a discipline: GDV − GST (margin scheme) − selling − build (with a market builder's margin for yourself INCLUDED) − demo − contributions (~$6.5–8.6k state HPC + council 7.11 on the net extra dwelling + Hunter Water headworks, now at 100% DSP) − interest − 5–10% contingency ⇒ residual land value. Only buy below residual. Target 18–20%+ margin on cost.
- **Find your finance-brained partner.** Gurner had Schwartz/Ciarma, Alcock had Webb & Brown-Neaves, Thirdi had Ron Dadd, Graya has Andrew's QS brain + STAC. Almost nobody in this research went solo. You own delivery; partner or hire the capital-structuring leg.

### The ladder (each rung funds the next)

| Rung | Years (indic.) | Move | Funding |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Now | Buy PPOR = duplex site (worst house, right block, LMR/Lake Mac) | Owner-occ loan, ~$500k capacity |
| 1 | 0–2 | Reno at cost → revalue → equity release. Option/delayed-settle a 2nd site if the feaso sings | Sweat equity |
| 2 | 2–4 | First KDR duplex (own block with tax advice, or site #2 in SPV). Build at cost, CDC pattern book, Torrens split. Sell one, keep one if rent covers debt | Equity release + construction loan; builder margin as part-equity |
| 3 | 4–7 | Rinse-repeat duplexes ×2–3, same corridor. Keep one side each time you can (Triguboff rule: never be a forced seller) | Recycled profits + resi/dev debt |
| 4 | 7–10 | Step to 3–6 dwelling townhouse/triplex projects. Start selling your own stock in-house (no REA licence needed for property you own — verify with Fair Trading before scaling). Landowner JVs to control sites without buying | Private credit + presales; JV equity |
| 5 | 10+ | The integrated brand: in-house design+sales, one flagship project that changes your tier, buy into (or marry) an agency à la Graya/WHITEFOX, hold a rent roll/management rights | Track record = fundable asset; consider a capital arm last |

The integration sequence, in the order the modern cohort actually did it: **brand → in-house sales → in-house construction (you start with this — your unfair advantage) → capital arm last.**

## Part 3 — The Rich List founders (largest → down), ranked by relevance to a tradie

### 1. Dale Alcock — ABN Group (WA) — closest match to your path
Apprentice **bricklayer 1979** (Kellerberrin wheatbelt); registered builder 1987; **partnered with his employers** (Webb & Brown-Neaves) — his name and work ethic, their balance sheet and systems. Scaled one volume brand into a multi-brand ladder, then integrated into ~20 companies: land, finance (Resolve), insurance, trade supply. Australia's largest privately owned construction/property/finance group in its markets; 21,000+ homes from the Dale Alcock brand. **Lesson: you don't need capital first — partner with people who have systems and balance sheet; then integrate one adjacent margin at a time, owning the customer's whole journey.**

### 2. Bob Ell — Leda Holdings — the Hunter carpenter who became a billionaire
Born **Merriwa, Hunter Valley (1945)**; carpentry apprentice in Sydney; founded Leda **1976** with simple industrial sheds — the ugliest product, where a builder's cost knowledge beats suit-wearing developers. Rolled profits into **corridor land banking** (Tweed/Kings Forest ~5,000+ lots, SW Sydney) decades before rezoning; holds shopping centres for income while land matures. ~$2.4B. **Lesson: start with simple stock you can build cheaply; park profits in growth-corridor dirt you can wait on.**

### 3. Harry Triguboff — Meriton (~$26–29B)
Milk run and taxis; **1963, first deal:** ~£3,400 Tempe land → 8-unit walk-up → sold in 8 months for £25,500. Bought back 100% of the company in 1973; when the 1970s downturn hit, **rented what wouldn't sell** and discovered the flywheel: rental income services debt, so he's never a forced seller and banks can't dictate terms. Now 10,000+ rental apartments + ~4,500 Meriton Suites; developer + builder + landlord + hotelier in one entity; buys sites without approvals (cheaper) and fights councils himself. **Lesson: the units you don't sell make you rich — hold whatever the rent will carry.**

### 4. Lang Walker — Walker Corp (d. 2024, ~$5B)
**1964: earthmoving/excavation with his dad** → subdivisions on land the machines were already cutting → apartments/industrial → CBD precincts (King St Wharf, Collins Square ~$2.5B, Parramatta Square ~$3B). **Sold the whole book at the top twice** (Australand 1999; ~$1.45B to Mirvac in 2006), sat in cash through the GFC, bought distressed. **Lesson: your plant/trade business is the seed developer — do the works for yourself, not a client; and be willing to sell everything when the market is stupid.**

### 5. Sam Arnaout — Iris Capital (~$2.6B) — the Newcastle proof
Western Sydney **panel beater**; first commercial buy Lidcombe Post Office 1998; built a pub empire — freehold land + gaming cashflow banks lend generously against — then the tier-changing deal: bought Newcastle's derelict **East End (1.66ha, 2016)** and delivered the ~$1B East End Village, Newcastle's biggest-ever residential project. Sold ~$500M of Hunter pubs (2022–25) to recycle into development. **Lesson: buy income first, development upside second — cash-flowing freeholds are a tradie-accessible bridge between wages and development. And Newcastle rewarded the developer who committed when the CBD was written off.**

### 6. Nigel Satterley — Satterley Property Group
Wheatbelt kid; jeans distributor at 20; **$10k profit on his first land block at 21**; banker-mentor Sir James McCusker backed him to build Statesman Homes → sold it 1980 → pivoted to **developing the land under the houses**. Now Australia's largest private residential land developer, mostly via **JVs with landowners, churches, super funds, state land agencies** — partners bring the dirt, he brings brand + delivery. **Lesson: builder margin is the apprenticeship; land is the wealth. A banker who trusts you is worth more than capital.**

### 7. Maha Sinnathamby — Springfield City Group
Civil engineer; near-broke repeatedly; **1992, age 52: 2,860ha of unwanted bush for $7.9M, largely borrowed**, only 10% zoned. Decades lobbying for rezoning, rail, university → Australia's only privately built city. **Lesson: the biggest margin in property is turning un-zoned land into zoned land; at your scale, the same alchemy is dual-occ → multi uplift.**

### 8. Tim Gurner — GURNER (~$900M+)
Age 19: $34k from grandfather + $12k savings → Elwood gym flip. Then the part he admits post-"avocado": **worked essentially free for Morry Schwartz, whose backing (and his boss's ~$180k) bought him into deals**; ~9 years as junior partner (Urban Inc with Danny Ciarma, ~1,300 dwellings) before founding GURNER at 30. Now BTR platform + wellness clubs + funds management — from developer margin to recurring fees on other people's money. **Lesson: apprentice yourself to a proven developer, even free — a patron compresses 20 years into 5.**

### 9–12. Historic templates, quick hits
- **Dick Dusseldorp (Lendlease):** ran contractor Civil & Civic; **1958 created Lend Lease purely to finance C&C's projects** — one entity doing design+construction+development+funding, later funds management. *Whoever controls the finance controls the project; build a funding arm eventually.*
- **John Roberts (Multiplex):** estimator/labourer → founded Multiplex 1962 on "on time, on budget" reliability → construction profits into development/hotels. Wembley nearly broke it. *Reliability is a monetisable brand; one bad fixed-price megaproject can undo 40 years.*
- **Grollo family (Grocon):** one concrete truck (1940s) → subcontractor → head contractor → Rialto/Eureka. Grocon (the builder) collapsed 2020; the family wealth survived **because the assets were held outside the builder**. *Separate the risk entity from the asset entity — always.*
- **Perich family:** 1951 dairy farm at Leppington, 25 cows; kept buying paddocks; Sydney grew to them (Oran Park). *On a growth city's fringe, the land under your business out-earns the business; never sell the freehold.*
- **Con Constantine (Newcastle, cautionary):** markets + Newcastle property from 1992; over-geared, receivership scare 2011. *Cashflow assets bought cheap work — until leverage and pet projects eat them.*

### The 7 patterns that repeat
1. **Construction margin is the first equity** — nobody started with capital; the licence is the cheat code.
2. **Other people's money, immediately** — borrowed or partnered from day one, against deals, not lifestyle.
3. **A patron/mentor with a balance sheet changes the timeline** — the fastest risers were sponsored, not solo.
4. **Buy dirt in the path of growth and wait** — rezoning/approval uplift dwarfs build margin.
5. **Never be a forced seller** — hold the stock that rents; downturns become buying seasons.
6. **One flagship precinct changes your tier** — East End for Arnaout is the live Newcastle example.
7. **Integrate vertically, then financially** — trade → builder → developer → landlord → financier; risk entity separate from asset entity.

### Pattern summary
1. First deal is always small and personally guaranteed. 2. A senior partner/patron appears in almost every story — find your Ciarma/Dadd. 3. Sequence: reno/flip → small infill → repeat in one precinct → precinct scale → hold assets. Nobody skipped steps except those who bought cash-flowing assets first. 4. **Regional is not a handicap** — McDonald, McCloy, Stevens, Arnaout and Thirdi built nine-to-ten-figure businesses substantially inside the market you're standing in.

### Graya-equivalents in other states
- **Cavcorp — Damien Cavallucci (Brisbane):** family builder → wellness-lifestyle brand; one suburb (Newstead) for 20+ years; in-house everything; engineered-to-be-photographed stunts (95m cantilevered pool).

### In-house sales economics
Project marketing/agency fees run **2–4% of gross realisation** — on a $100M project that's $2–2.5M, often a third of the developer's net margin — recycled into a team that also owns the buyer database, controls price integrity across staged releases (+2–5%/stage), and feeds the next project's presale covenant. Mosaic extends furthest downstream (management rights = recurring revenue + resale listings).

### The repeatable structural playbook
1. **Integration sequence: brand → in-house sales → construction → capital arm last** (only once your track record is itself a fundable asset).
2. **One geography, compounding brand** — the suburb becomes the moat and off-market deal flow comes to you.
3. **Brand-led presales replace project marketers** — media is a financing instrument.
4. **Own or marry the funding** — private credit relationships are the growth-ceiling breaker (with governance risk).
5. **Sell to owner-occupiers/downsizers** — settlement reliability and word-of-mouth.
6. **Design as differentiation, trade skill as credibility** — exactly the trade-origin founders' lane (Willing, Cavallucci, Rob Gray).

## Part 6 — The "gatekept" small-developer playbook

### §1 The ecosystem and what it consistently teaches
- **Rob Flux — Property Developer Network:** ~15,000 members, monthly meetups, 12-month mentoring. Nine-step process; "3 pathways in" (investor, renovator, **tradie**); "**7 ways to profit**" — you can exit at multiple points (raw site flip, DA flip, build-through, develop-and-hold), not just the end.
- **Justin Gehde — Property Developer Podcast:** first deal should be the one you can *finance and survive*; build your consultant team before your first site.
- **Little Fish Properties (Melb):** unusually transparent duplex numbers; hammer holding-cost erosion; "cheap suburbs kill returns" — build cost is flat but end values aren't. Corollary: **worst house, good suburb**.
- **Duayne Pearce:** builders' #1 development mistake is importing their underquoting habit into their own feasibility.
- Property Council Academy / UDIA NSW run formal courses — worth one for the feasibility/finance vocabulary lenders expect.

**The consistent message:** builders have cost knowledge and delivery but fail on *acquisition discipline, finance structuring and tax* — so: buy on residual land value, know your exit before you buy, never price your own labour at zero.

### §2 The standard first development
- **Why duplex first:** smallest project where the economics work (one DA, shared structure, two sales), equity can be ~20% of TDC (vs 30%+ multi-unit), no/low presales needed, builder can self-deliver.
- **Numbers (NSW 2025-26):** margin target 15–25% on total development cost (18–20% floor; below 15% = no buffer). Duplex build $2,195–$2,455/m² medium spec (Rawlinsons-based), market range to $3,800/m²; 2×150m² ≈ $600–840k. **Torrens subdivision adds ~$30–60k vs strata but materially lifts end values.** Contingency 5–10% minimum even when you're the builder.
- **Classic mistakes:** pricing own labour at zero (the "profit" is just unpaid wages — market builder's margin goes IN the feaso, development margin stacks on top); no contingency/holding line; **tax surprises** — a builder doing a duplex is almost certainly on revenue account (ordinary income, no 50% CGT discount; the ATO decides on intention), **GST applies to new residential** with the margin scheme routinely worth tens of thousands per dwelling (must be in the contract, eligibility depends on how you acquired); **main-residence trap** — KDR your own home into two-to-sell and you likely lose the exemption; get specialist advice before demolishing; **structure set up after purchase** = duty/CGT to fix.

### §3 The financing ladder
- Resi-style lending + equity at duplex scale (cheapest); development finance is project-assessed (DA+CC, fixed-price contract — awkward when you're the builder; expect QS verification), banks ~65% GRV / 80% TDC with presales; **non-banks/private credit: 65–75% GRV, 75–80% TDC, zero presales on strong duplex deals** at ~9–12%+ (verify current).
- **Builder's sweat equity:** the builder's margin within the build contract can be treated as part of the equity contribution — the builder's structural advantage; treatment varies by lender.
- **JVs:** landowner JV (owner contributes site, you fund/deliver, split ~50/50 after land credit); money-partner JV (pref return + split waterfall); builder-as-equity JV (deliver at cost for equity). Use an SPV + unitholders/JV agreement, never a partnership. Margin scheme flows through landowner JVs advantageously.

### §4 Vertical integration & structure
- **Selling your own stock:** NSW Property & Stock Agents Act licensing attaches to acting *for others for reward* — selling property you/your SPV own is not agency work, so **no REA licence needed for your own developments** (confirm the Fair Trading exemption in writing before scaling an in-house sales function; employee-marketing and JV-lot sales raise questions).
- **Structure:** SPV per project (Pty Ltd, sometimes trustee of a trust); separate builder entity contracts to the SPV; director guarantees pierce the SPV so **quarantine the family home** and avoid cross-collateralising it into the development facility; set up before exchange.

### §5 Documented tricks of the trade
1. **Call / put-and-call options:** control a site for a small fee, run the DA during the option period, complete or **nominate/on-sell the option to capture DA uplift without double stamp duty** (NSW). The single most-taught insider acquisition tool.
2. **Long/delayed settlement + vendor consent to lodge the DA during settlement** — settle with approval in hand having risked only the deposit.
3. **DA-uplift-and-flip vs build-through:** an approved duplex site is worth materially more than raw; a builder's edge is the build, so build-through usually wins for you — but the flip exit exists if capital gets thin.
4. **LMR policy targeting:** since Feb 2025 — dual occ on 450m²/12m frontage/FSR 0.65/9.5m as non-discretionary standards near nominated stations/centres; **Torrens splits at 225m²/6m width**; **corner blocks can use the secondary frontage for the 12m rule.** Old fibro on flat, sewer-friendly ~450m²+ corner lots inside LMR rings is the classic target.
5. **Buy the worst house:** structurally-shot stock priced as "renovator" but valued as land in your residual model — the spread is the entry profit.
