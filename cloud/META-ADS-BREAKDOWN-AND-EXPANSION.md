**What both share:** strong offer formula (price/timeframe + fixed price + transformation proof).
**What both miss:** they cover **2 of your ~10 residential lines**, and both are cold-audience only — no retarget (warm) or lookalike (past-customer seed). That's where the cheap leads are.

## The winning formula (reuse it for every job type)
`[transformation timelapse OR before/after] + price-or-consult offer + "fixed price" + "I'll text you back within the hour" + suburb geo + Meta Lead Form + Lic 477499C + 100% Aboriginal-owned`

Two offer styles — pick by job size:
- **Fixed-scope jobs → price + timeframe hook** ("$X, Y days, fixed price"): bathroom, kitchen,
  laundry, deck, pergola, carport, small interior/exterior reno.
- **Big/variable jobs → consult + quote-turnaround hook** (no fixed $): granny flat, new build,
  extension, additions, whole-home reno.

## How to roll it out (don't dump 8 ads on a $2k/mo budget)
1. **Sequence, don't blast.** Add the next 2 highest-demand + media-ready lines first — likely
   **Kitchen** and **Granny flat** (granny flat = high-margin, IPP + investor angle, strong demand).
2. Run each new concept as a **creative variation inside the existing Cold set** first (no new
   budget), 2 at a time, 7-day test, ≥30% winner stays (per the program doc).
3. **Then** split into **Cold / Retarget / Lookalike** ad sets so each job type runs to warm +
   past-customer audiences (the cheap leads).
4. **Capture the missing media** on the next kitchen / granny flat / new-build job — use
   `koori_notes.py`-style capture + the fleet's photo-categoriser to build the before/during/after
   + timelapse library so every line has a proof asset.
5. Route every job type's leads to a **per-type Lead Form** (or one form with a "job type" field)
   so you can see CPL and quote→win **by job type** — that tells you where to put budget.

**Watch each with the change-triggers** in `META-ADS-PROGRAM-AND-GAPS.md` (CPL >$50/7d → swap
creative; frequency >2.5 → rotate; lots of leads/few booked → tighten the form).
